# Sponsorship policy

## When sponsorship becomes possible

No cash sponsorship before **three completed events** and a stable culture. An
in-kind venue partnership is acceptable earlier, but only with no promotional
obligation attached.

Regular membership, tech hangouts, and normal build sessions stay free.

## What a sponsor may cover

Venue, refreshments, equipment, or one defined activity.

## What a sponsor gets

A logo and a brief thank-you. That is the whole list.

## What a sponsor never gets

- Member or attendance data, in any form
- Lead collection at an event
- A compulsory pitch or sales presentation
- Control over topics, membership, speakers, or rules
- A misleading endorsement
- Category exclusivity that harms the community

## Categories declined outright

Political organizations, gambling, predatory courses, adult products, and
questionable financial or crypto promotions.

## Review checklist

Every sponsorship passes all nine before acceptance:

- [ ] Fits an age-open technology community
- [ ] Exact benefit to members is named
- [ ] Exact money or in-kind value is named
- [ ] Will be publicly disclosed
- [ ] No data access
- [ ] No content or membership control
- [ ] No more than a brief acknowledgement
- [ ] Approved by two organizers
- [ ] Written agreement, with an exit right for Lambda House

## Disclosure

Sponsored events and sponsored spending are disclosed publicly, at the event and
in the recap.

## Paid workshops

May be considered later, only where there is a real instructor, materials, or
venue cost. Paid activity never becomes the path to belonging, and a free-seat
mechanism always remains available.
